Where it could live

Three ways to place it. The student side is the same question card in each; what changes is where the teacher works and how close it sits to Did I.

A · INSIDE DID I

A Classes section in the app

Teachers and students both use Did I. Classes is one more menu item.

  • Good: one app, one account, it reuses everything.
  • Bad: teacher tools crowd an app built to be calm. A teenager's own questions sit next to school ones, which can feel like school is watching.
B · ITS OWN APP

A separate Did I Class app

Teachers and students download Did I Class. Did I stays personal.

  • Good: clean split, own store listing, can sell to schools without touching Did I.
  • Bad: a second app to build, review and support. Students who already use Did I get school questions in a different place.
C · RECOMMENDED

Teachers on the web and phone, students in Did I

Teachers plan on a laptop at did-i.app/class and can ask or check from a small Teaching section in Did I on their phone. Students join with a code and the questions land in Did I under their own "class" tag, which they can mute or leave.

  • Good: teachers live on laptops; no teacher tools in the app; students get one more tag, not a new app. Cheap to test.
  • Bad: students need Did I installed; school stays a little inside the personal app (kept in its own tag).

Teacher: the web dashboard (option C)

Make a class, share the code, post a question for a night, see the roster. Students show as the name they choose; no emails or phone numbers.

1. Who it is for

RoleWhoWhere they work
Student13 and up: high school and collegeDid I on their phone (tonight, widget, push)
TeacherOwns the class, asks questions, sees the rosterWeb (did-i.app/class) and a Teaching section in Did I
Co-teacher / TAInvited by the teacher; can ask, remind, see the roster, give extensions; cannot delete the class, change billing or remove the ownerSame as teacher
School admin (phase 3)Buys Pro for teachers, sees which teachers have it, no student dataWeb only
Did I staff (you)Reviews reports, freezes or removes classesPrivate admin page

Anyone can hold more than one role: a college TA is a student in one class and a co-teacher in another. Each class shows in the right place.

Not for: under 13, parents watching their kids (that is Family), employers watching staff. Parents get no access to a student's classes. The sign-up, the join screen and the store listing say who it is for.

2. The student

Joining

  • Three ways in: a link (did-i.app/c/CODE), a QR code on the board or projector, or typing the code.
  • Codes: 8 characters from letters and numbers that cannot be confused (no O/0, I/1), shown as BIO-4KX7. Wrong codes are limited per account per day (the family-code pattern) so codes cannot be guessed.
  • Before joining they see: class name, the teacher's display name, number of students, and the plain promise: "Your teacher sees only whether you answered class questions (Did, Didn't, Excused, Not yet). Not when, not your own questions, not your streak. Classmates see nothing about you."
  • Age check: a one-time "I'm 13 or older" if the account has not confirmed it. Under 13 cannot join, and the app says so kindly.
  • Name in class: they choose how they show up in each class (prefilled with their display name, for example first name and last initial). No email, phone or friend ID is ever shared.
  • No account yet: the link opens the store, then the app picks the code up on first launch (the invite-link pickup already does this). Joining works signed in only, so answers follow them to a new phone.
  • Teacher approval (optional): with "Approve who joins" on, joins wait; the student sees "Waiting for your teacher to let you in".
  • Reminders nudge: if notifications are off, the join screen asks once to turn them on, and explains it is the only way to get class questions on time.

On their phone

  • Class questions are normal Did I questions under the class's own tag (class color, class name as the tag). Same swipe, widget, reminders, and "Not yet can still become Did".
  • They arrive at the time the teacher picked, with Did / Not yet buttons on the notification.
  • Due dates: "by Friday" questions stay on tonight each night until answered or until the due date passes, then close as Didn't. There is no Late: a student either did it or didn't (decided 2026-10-08).
  • Repeating questions use the existing weekday and every-N schedules.
  • A link (optional) sits under the question. The domain shows before they tap ("opens docs.google.com"). Links open in the browser, never inside the app.
  • Anonymous check-ins (v1): some questions say "Anonymous: your teacher sees only totals", for things like "Did today's lesson make sense?". The answer is never stored against their name on the teacher's side. The system still knows who has answered (so remind skips them) but never what they answered.
  • Streaks: class questions count toward their own streak only if they turn on "Count toward my streak" for that class. Off by default.

Their own record

  • Class history (v1): per class, what they did, missed or were excused from this term, the same statuses the teacher sees.
  • "What my teacher sees": one tap shows their own row exactly as the teacher's roster shows it. No surprises.
  • Export: their class answers are included in the existing "Save a copy".

Control (the student is always in charge of their phone)

  • Mute a class tonight or for a week (still answerable inside the tag; no pushes, not on the widget).
  • Quiet hours always win; a class question asked inside them waits until they end.
  • Leave a class any time. Its questions leave their tonight. The teacher sees "left", nothing more.
  • Report a class or a teacher (spam, harassment, not a real class): it leaves the class and flags it for review.
  • Show my picture to the teacher: off by default, per class.

3. The teacher

Making a class

  • Name, optional section ("Period 3", "Lecture A"), subject color, term dates, time zone (ask times follow the class zone).
  • No-class days: mark holidays and breaks by hand or start from a simple preset (US federal holidays); repeating questions skip them.
  • Duplicate a class for a new term or section (questions and schedule copy; students do not).
  • Join material: a link, a code, a QR, a full-screen projector slide and a printable poster (PDF, one page: class name, QR, code, the privacy promise in one line).

Asking (phone and web)

  • Type the rest of "Did you…" (up to 120 characters).
  • Classes: one or several at once.
  • When: now, tonight 7 PM, tomorrow 7:30 AM, or pick a time.
  • Due: just that night, or by a date.
  • Repeat: none, weekdays, specific days, every N days, until a date or the end of term.
  • Anonymous (v1): totals only.
  • Link: optional, https only.
  • Preview line: "74 students get "Did I do the chapter 5 reading?" Tue and Thu at 7 PM, until the term ends."
  • Edit or take back: before it is asked, anything; after, fix a typo or take it back (it leaves students' tonight; it never counts).
  • Question bank (Pro): past questions, one tap to ask again, plus the starter questions.
  • Plan the week (Pro): schedule a week of questions on the laptop.

Seeing who did it

  • Statuses: Did, Didn't, Excused, Not yet. Each has a word and an icon as well as a color.
  • Small classes (up to about 40): a list of names with statuses.
  • Big classes: totals first ("241 of 300 did it", a bar, the counts), the remind button, then names one tap away with search and filters.
  • Term grid (web, Pro): each student across the term; "joined later" is its own mark, never Didn't.
  • Never shown: when they answered, their own questions, tags, streak, other classes, whether they opened the app, anonymous answers by person.
  • Export (web, Pro): CSV of the grid (class name of each student and statuses).

Accommodations and excuses

  • Extend for one student: a later due date for a single student on a question or for the whole term (students with formal accommodation plans such as IEP or 504 plans need this). Their question stays open until their own due date. Only the teacher and co-teachers see that an extension exists; the student just sees their own due date.
  • Excuse: mark a student Excused for a question (absent, special case). Excused never counts as Didn't.

Reminding

  • Remind the ones who haven't: one push to students still on Not yet. Once per question per night, never inside quiet hours, never to students who muted the class. The button says how many it reaches and greys out after.
  • Always "everyone who hasn't", never one student by name.

Managing

  • Approve joins, remove a student (they get a quiet notice), co-teachers by friend ID or link, new code (old one stops working).
  • End of term: archive. It leaves every student's tonight; the teacher keeps a read-only roster for 1 year, then it is deleted.
  • Delete class: removes it and all its answers for everyone, after a confirm that says exactly that.
  • Teacher's own Did I stays separate: their personal questions never mix with Teaching.

Teaching section in the app

  • Shown in the menu only to people who teach a class.
  • Home: classes with tonight's progress; "Ask a class something".
  • Ask, Who did it, Remind, Extend/Excuse, Class settings (projector, approve joins, co-teachers, term, archive).
  • Heavier work (bank, week plan, grid, export, billing, poster) is on the web.

4. Onboarding

Teachers

  1. Sign up on the web or in the app (same account; email code, Apple or Google). One question: "Are you teaching a class?" opens Teaching.
  2. Make your first class in a three-step walkthrough: name, term, time zone.
  3. Starter questions to pick from: "Did you do the reading?", "Did you bring your lab notebook?", "Did you study for Friday's quiz?", "Did you turn in the permission slip?", "Did you finish the problem set?", "Did you watch the lecture?", "Did you check the class page?", "Did you sign up for a presentation slot?", "Did you charge your laptop for tomorrow?", "Did you read the feedback on your draft?".
  4. Try it as a student: a preview phone shows exactly what students get, including the join screen promise.
  5. Get students in: projector slide, poster, link to paste in the class page.
  6. First result: after the first question's night, an email and in-app card: "31 of 34 did it. Here is your roster."
  7. Empty roster shows the join code and poster, not a blank table.

Students

  • Joining is the onboarding: the class card appears on tonight right away with a one-time hint "Swipe right when you did it". People new to Did I also get the normal first-run (the first-swipe opening).

5. Privacy, age and safety

The rule: a teacher sees one thing about a student: the status of each class question. Everything follows from that.

Age and audience

  • 13+ only; under 13 cannot join. Keeps clear of COPPA and the App Store Kids category. The App Store age rating questionnaire is answered again for the update.
  • One way: students cannot post, reply, message or see classmates.
  • No parents in a student class: a student class gives no one outside it any view of a student. Parents take part only through a parent class (below).

Parent classes (elementary and middle school, added 2026-10-08)

For grades where students are under 13, the teacher picks "Parents and guardians answer" when making the class. Built so that no child is ever involved:

  • Only adults join. Joining asks "I'm a parent or guardian, 18 or older". No child account, no child sign-in, no child age, birthday or photo.
  • No child is named. The parent joins under their own name ("your name, not your child's"). The teacher matches families to kids outside the app, the way they already know which parent is whose.
  • Questions are about the household: the teacher writes "Did your family read together for 15 minutes?"; the parent sees "Did we read together for 15 minutes?" on Tonight, the widget and reminders.
  • The teacher sees one status per family: Did, Didn't, Excused, Not yet. Never when. Remind, Excuse and "+ nights" work the same, aimed at parents.
  • Both parents can join; the household is one row (shared through the existing Family link), and the first answer counts for both.
  • Middle school: the teacher chooses per class, students (13+) or parents.
  • Why this keeps us clear: nothing is collected from or about a named child, so COPPA's child rules do not apply; the lawyer review still covers FERPA and state student-privacy laws, since it is still a school use.
  • Fairness: a family that never joined shows as "not joined", not Didn't. Excused stays one tap.

What is shared

  • Class display name (student's choice) and statuses. Picture only on the student's opt-in. Nothing else, ever.
  • Anonymous check-ins are stored without a link to the student on the teacher's side (counts only).

Law and policy (lawyer review before the pilot)

  • US student records (FERPA): a teacher signing up alone controls their class records; a school-bought plan needs a data agreement (phase 3).
  • US state student-privacy laws (California and others) cover apps used in K-12. Have a lawyer check the pilot setup before any high school uses it.
  • GDPR / data rights: students can delete their account (class answers go too); teachers can export and delete a class.
  • Terms of use: a teacher section with acceptable use (real classes only, no harassment, no collecting data off-platform, no ads).
  • Privacy policy, App Store privacy labels and Play data safety updated before launch.
  • Retention: archived classes kept 1 year, then deleted. Students who leave are dropped from the teacher's view at archive.

Safety and abuse

  • Caps: classes made per account per week, questions per class per day, 300 members, one remind per question per night, text lengths, wrong-code attempts per day.
  • Per-student push cap: across all classes, at most 3 class pushes a day per student; more fold into one "3 class questions tonight" push.
  • Text and links: questions up to 120 characters, a basic word filter, links https only with the domain shown, links to known bad sites blocked (Google Safe Browsing check when the question is saved).
  • Impersonation: the join screen always shows the teacher's account name; students can report "not my teacher".
  • Reports: a reported class over a threshold is frozen (no new questions) until reviewed; see section 10.
  • No teacher verification at launch; caps, reports and one-way design are the protection. A verified-school badge comes with the school plan.

7. Accessibility, language and states

Accessibility

  • Screen-reader labels on every control; the roster reads "Student 12, Not yet".
  • Statuses use a word and an icon, not color alone (color-blind safe).
  • Large text sizes reflow; nothing is cut off at the biggest size.
  • The swipe has the existing tap alternatives; reduced motion respected.

Language

  • Every app and dashboard string in all 9 locales. Teachers' question text is shown as written (not translated).

States (each designed, not left blank)

  • Class with no students yet: code, QR, poster, "share this".
  • Student with no classes: "Join a class" with code entry.
  • Wrong code, code from a deleted class, full class (300), archived class, waiting for approval, removed from a class, class frozen.
  • Offline: answers save on the phone and sync later (existing behaviour); the roster shows "last updated" when the teacher is offline.
  • Dark and light themes, every look.

9. Support, help and marketing

  • Help page for teachers (did-i.app/class/help): getting started, what students see, privacy, extensions, billing, FAQ.
  • Contact: a support email linked from the dashboard and the app; aim to answer within 2 school days.
  • Landing page (did-i.app/class): what it is in one line, the privacy promise, the student view, free vs Pro, a demo class to click through, the poster.
  • Demo class: a read-only sample class with a filled roster, so a teacher can see the product before making anything.
  • Store listings: a screenshot and a line about classes once it ships.

10. Admin and moderation (Did I staff)

  • Private admin page (signed-in staff only, never in the app): reported classes with the reason, their questions, member count, report count; actions: unfreeze, remove class, ban teacher account.
  • Admin never sees student answers or names beyond what the report needs.
  • Every admin action is logged (who, when, what).
  • Thresholds: a class is frozen automatically at 3 reports from different students or 10% of members, whichever comes first.

11. How it is built (reusing what exists)

  • Closest existing feature: Did we (supabase/did-we.sql): shared cards, members, answers, nudges, join links. Classes are new tables built the same way, not a change to Did we.
  • Tables: classes, class_members (role, class display name, muted until, left at, show picture, count toward streak), class_questions (text, ask time, schedule, due, link, anonymous, taken back), class_answers (status per question per night per student; answered-at kept for night math only, never returned to teachers), class_extensions, class_excused, class_anon_counts, class_reminds (the once-a-night cap), class_reports, class_invites, class_code_misses, class_admin_log.
  • RLS on every table, (select auth.uid()), security definer helpers with pinned search_path ("am I teaching this class", the roster read). The roster function returns display name and status only.
  • Caps in the database (triggers): members, classes per week, questions per day, reminds, text sizes, code misses, per-student daily pushes.
  • On the phone: class questions are pulled like Did we cards and shown as lamps in the class tag; tonight, widget, reminders and notification buttons already handle them.
  • Push: the existing push queue, sent in batches so a 300-student ask goes out within a minute without hitting provider limits.
  • Web dashboard: a new area of the website, same account and sign-in.
  • Billing: Stripe Checkout and the customer portal; a webhook updates the teacher's plan in the database.
  • Milestones (docs/milestones.md): class_made, class_joined, class_asked, class_reminded.
  • Monitoring: push delivery failures, roster query time, report rate, error reports, all on a simple admin view.
  • Locales: all 9.
  • Tests: RLS isolation (students never read rosters; a teacher never reads another class), caps, one remind a night, under-13 refused, answer times never returned to teachers, anonymous answers never linked, extension maths.

12. Edge cases (decided)

  • Student in two classes from the same teacher: two tags.
  • Same question to two sections: one ask, several classes.
  • Ask time changed after asking: students who already got it keep it.
  • Student changes phones: answers are on the account.
  • Notifications off: questions still show in the app and widget.
  • Joins mid-term: earlier questions show "joined later".
  • Time zones: ask times follow the class zone; never inside quiet hours.
  • Daylight saving and midnight: the same night math as the rest of Did I.
  • Teacher leaves or deletes their account: a co-teacher becomes owner; with none, the class archives.
  • Code leaked: new code, approval on, remove members.
  • Archived while a question is open: it closes with nothing recorded.
  • Taken-back question: never counts.
  • Student with two accounts joins twice: the teacher can remove one.
  • Student leaves and rejoins: their earlier statuses come back.
  • Teacher downgrades with 3 classes: 2 go read-only; students keep answering questions already scheduled until they end.
  • Anonymous question turned non-anonymous: not allowed after it is asked.
  • Class at 300: the join screen says the class is full and to ask the teacher.

13. What it deliberately does not do

  • No grades or gradebook, no assignments or file uploads.
  • No chat, comments or student posts.
  • No tracking when or how often a student opens the app.
  • No parent access.
  • No LMS sync at launch (Google Classroom / Canvas roster import is phase 3, if asked).
  • No ads, ever, and nothing sold about students.

14. Phases

  1. Pilot (about 6 weeks of work): make a class, join (link, code, QR, poster, projector), ask once or due by, tonight/widget/push, roster with statuses, remind, extend and excuse, mute, leave, report, archive, caps and RLS, 13+ check, onboarding with starter questions and student preview, per-student push cap, text and link limits, empty and error states, accessibility, admin review page, updated terms and privacy policy, lawyer check. Web dashboard and Teaching section. Free.
  2. v1: repeat schedules, links, co-teachers, anonymous check-ins, student class history, question bank, plan the week, term grid, CSV export, big lecture view, Pro billing, trial, help page, landing page, demo class, teacher referral.
  3. Schools: school plan, invoices, admin page, data agreement, verified badge, LMS roster import if asked.

Cheapest test before any of this: a Did we card marked as a class card (join by link, the maker sees the roster, members cannot see each other).

15. Pilot plan

  • Who: 3 to 5 teachers: at least one high school class, one college lecture over 100, one small seminar.
  • How long: 4 weeks of real use.
  • Check-ins: a 15-minute call each week and a short form for students (5 questions, optional, anonymous).
  • Go to v1 if: most teachers are still asking in week 4, students answer at least 70% of class questions, mute and leave rates stay under 10%, and no serious safety report.
  • Stop or rethink if: teachers stop asking by week 2, or students mute most classes.

16. How we know it works

  • Teachers: class made → first question within a day → still asking in week 3.
  • Students: joined → answered the first class question → still answering in week 3; mute and leave rates low.
  • Per question: share answered by the due date (a good class is above 80%).
  • Reports per 1,000 students near zero.
  • Teachers convert to Pro when they make a second class.

19. Decided (2026-10-07)
